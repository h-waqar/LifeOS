Design quality is high, implementation risk is concentrated in one place. T-22-04 is the sharpest edge: Twitter rotates refresh tokens on use, so two concurrent workers refreshing the same token can permanently disconnect the account. Gaps, drift and risks worth knowing about

1 · Planning-state drift around Phase 22. state.json lists phase 22 as pending while its own next.reason says 0% · executing; ROADMAP.md shows 0/2 · Not started even though both plans exist; STATE.md header says status: executing while the body says "READY TO EXECUTE". Four artifacts, three different readings. Harmless today, but a resume lands on an ambiguous state.
2 · Plan-count mismatch. Adding the roadmap rows gives 75 delivered plans (41 + 27 + 7), while STATE.md reports "Total plans completed: 73". One of the two is wrong; the roadmap is the more granular source.
3 · Nyquist validation is drafted, not enforced. 22-VALIDATION.md is status: draft with nyquist_compliant: false and all six Wave-0 test stubs unchecked. Phases 19–21 have no validation strategy file at all. Nothing blocks execution, but the per-task verification map is currently a plan rather than a promise.
4 · 72 integration tests are unverified in this environment. They cover the security-critical paths — real PostgreSQL transactions, FOR UPDATE concurrency, fail-closed behavior. They need a running database before Phase 22 touches credential storage.
5 · No social platform has ever been connected. The OAuth paths in Phases 22–24 are greenfield. Real X and LinkedIn app credentials, redirect URIs, rate-limit tiers and webhook registration are environmental unknowns until an account exists.
